# Walkthrough: a product-incident impact task, resolved end to end

This narrates one real resolution over the seed graph, from task descriptor to context pack to Twin Composition Snapshot, in the five steps of the resolver algorithm. The input is [`task-product-impact.json`](task-product-impact.json). The two output fixtures it produces, [`expected/task-product-impact.pack.json`](expected/task-product-impact.pack.json) and [`expected/task-product-impact.snapshot.json`](expected/task-product-impact.snapshot.json), are **generated by running the resolver and are compared by the resolver-determinism CI check**. They are not hand-written and their content is not reproduced here; this document describes their shape and points at them. A proposal that changes resolution must regenerate them, and a stale fixture is a red CI check.

How the fixtures are produced, and how to reproduce them locally:

```
python resolver/resolve.py \
  --registry . \
  --task examples/task-product-impact.json \
  --registry-ref git:<commit> \
  --observed-at 2026-08-10T00:00:00Z \
  --out-pack examples/expected/task-product-impact.pack.json \
  --out-snapshot examples/expected/task-product-impact.snapshot.json
```

The resolver reads no wall clock and touches no network at resolve time. Every timestamp comes from a CLI argument (`--observed-at`, carried into both the pack `observed_at` and the snapshot `created_at`) or from a field in the input; `snapshot_id` is derived deterministically by hashing the resolved set together with the edges digest and the task reference; `edges_hash` is `"sha256:"` followed by the SHA-256 of the canonicalized `mmdg/edges.json` (sorted keys, no insignificant whitespace). Same inputs, byte-identical outputs, on every machine, which is exactly what lets CI regenerate the fixtures and diff them.

## The task

The descriptor validates against `schema/task-descriptor.schema.json`. It asks the resolver to assess the impact of a product incident across two domains at once: the software the affected product is built from, and the staffing roles accountable for the processes that cross products. It names `plmm.product` and `aismm.software-product` in `referenced_entities`, sets `requester_identity` to `agent:reference-runtime`, budgets `max_tokens` at 8000, and requires freshness within `PT24H`.

Note what the descriptor does **not** name: APMM. The staffing side is reached by the resolver walking the graph, not by the task naming it. This is the point of the MMDG. The task names an entity that a Landscape owns, and the Landscape's own edges pull in the Core models it composes and references.

## Step 1, route by declared hints

The resolver matches the descriptor against the `routing_hints` declared by the registered records: a hint matches when a `referenced_entities` entry appears in a record's `match_entities`, or the `purpose` contains one of its `match_purpose_keywords`. `entries/vercy.plmm.yaml` declares `match_entities` including `plmm.product` and `match_purpose_keywords` including terms such as `cross-product`. The referenced entity `plmm.product` appears in PLMM's `match_entities`, so the anchor set is `{vercy.plmm}`. The other referenced entity, `aismm.software-product`, names a kind AISMM exports but matches no declared routing hint in the seed (AISMM registers no `routing_hints`), so it selects nothing in step 1; AISMM enters the resolution through the walk in step 2. Every match is logged into the pack's `provenance.routing_log`. In v0.1 hint matching is the only routing stage; embedding fallback and human escalation are deferred.

## Step 2, walk the graph

The resolver takes the transitive closure of `composes` and `references` edges out of the anchor set, per the ARCH-017 walk discipline extended across models. From `vercy.plmm`:

- `vercy.plmm` **composes** `vercy.aismm` (declared_min 3.1.0, R2, kinds software-product and entity). This is the software side.
- `vercy.plmm` **references** `vercy.apmm` (declared_min 0.1.0, R4, kinds role and task). This is the staffing side, reached without the task naming APMM.

Nothing further is reachable. AISMM and APMM declare empty `requires`; the kernel `vercy.elmm` holds no outgoing edges. The seed draws no `deprecated-in-favor-of` or `masters-link` edge, so no successor pointer or mastership pointer participates in the walk.

## Step 3, Minimal Version Selection

For each reached model major identity the resolver takes the maximum of the declared minimums on participating incoming edges; an anchor with no participating incoming edge resolves to its registered version. `declared_min` on the edges is the sole input. The result is exactly one live instance per model:

| Model | Declared minimums | Resolved |
|---|---|---|
| `vercy.plmm` | anchor, no participating incoming edge | 0.2.0 (registered version) |
| `vercy.aismm` | 3.1.0 (edge from vercy.plmm) | 3.1.0 |
| `vercy.apmm` | 0.1.0 (edge from vercy.plmm) | 0.1.0 |

No solver runs; the outcome is deterministic and identical on every machine. Declared ranges played no part: any upper bound was already checked at publish time by the CI V-gates and is never a resolver input. Major versions are distinct identities, so had two majors of a shared Core model been reachable, each would resolve independently rather than competing for one slot.

## Step 4, pin at the declared ref

Each resolved model is pinned at the `source.ref` recorded in its registration record (v0.2.0, v3.1.0, v0.1.0), and its declared Semantic Fingerprint is read from the record as the semantic pin. The v0.1 skeleton opens no source repository and recomputes no fingerprint at resolve time; the live fetch and recompute named in the spec algorithm is deferred. `observed_at` is stamped from the `--observed-at` argument. Each record declares `sync_contract: {mode: git, freshness: PT24H}`; the stamped `observed_at` is within both the declared freshness and the descriptor's `PT24H` requirement, so no staleness flag is raised. Had `observed_at` been older than a source's declared freshness, the pack would still be emitted, carrying a staleness flag and the last-known `observed_at`, and the consumer would decide.

## Step 5, assemble, emit, and snapshot

The resolver builds one member projection per anchored Meta-Object, each satisfying the CORE-009 single-object rule and carrying its own provenance, enforces `max_tokens`, and emits the pack and the snapshot.

### The context pack (shape only; see the generated fixture)

`expected/task-product-impact.pack.json` validates against `schema/context-pack.schema.json`. Its shape:

- `task_ref`: `task-product-impact-0001`, carried from the descriptor.
- `member_projections`: an ordered array of members, each with `model_id`, a single `anchor` (the namespace-qualified CSN of the one Meta-Object it projects), a `form` of `full` or `summary`, the projected `content`, per-member `provenance` (the model version, its Semantic Fingerprint, and the pinned ref), and a `tokens` estimate. The expected resolution yields three members, each delivered in `summary` form under the just-in-time dereference rule (identifiers plus short summaries, with detail pulled on demand): `plmm.product` (the primary anchor), `aismm.software-product` (the software the product depends on), and `apmm.role` (the staffing roles that own the affected cross-product processes). The v0.1 resolver emits every member in summary form; `full` form is reserved for a future capability that projects content in depth when the budget and the minimal-sufficient rule call for it.
- `coverage_statement`: what the pack covers, what was delivered in summary form, whether any source was stale, and whether the budget forced degradation.
- `version_pins`: one pin per resolved model (`vercy.plmm` 0.2.0, `vercy.aismm` 3.1.0, `vercy.apmm` 0.1.0), each with its Semantic Fingerprint. The pins are the result of Minimal Version Selection; the fingerprint is the semantic pin, never a byte hash.
- `provenance`: `registry_ref` (the commit read), `resolver` (implementation and version), and `routing_log` (the hint matches, the edge walk, and the version selection, one line each).
- `observed_at`: from `--observed-at`.
- `max_tokens`: 8000. If the assembled members exceeded it, members would degrade to a terser identifier-plus-summary form and the drop would be recorded in `coverage_statement`; that is the entire degradation rule in v0.1.
- `exclusions`: what was deliberately left out and why, for example the kernel record (least privilege, and reached by no walkable edge) and the APMM kinds the task did not reference (minimal-sufficient).
- `requester_identity` and `purpose`: carried from the descriptor for purpose limitation and audit. In this single-trust-domain example no source-entitlement mapping is declared, so the projection builder evaluates none; the enforcement point is named for when one is.

The pack is read-only. Any agent-initiated mutation would leave through the propose-only write path toward the mastering system, not through this pack.

### The Twin Composition Snapshot (shape only; see the generated fixture)

`expected/task-product-impact.snapshot.json` validates against `schema/twin-snapshot.schema.json`. Its shape:

- `snapshot_id`: derived deterministically by hashing the resolved set with the edges digest and the task reference. Never random, never reused.
- `resolved`: one entry per resolved model major identity (`vercy.plmm` 0.2.0, `vercy.aismm` 3.1.0, `vercy.apmm` 0.1.0), each pinning `id`, `version`, and `semantic_fingerprint`, and optionally recording `source_ref` as the pinned ref.
- `edges_hash`: `"sha256:"` followed by the SHA-256 of the canonicalized `mmdg/edges.json`. A clearly labeled non-semantic transport-integrity field, never a semantic pin.
- `created_at`: from `--observed-at`.
- `task_ref`: `task-product-impact-0001`.

The replay property is what makes the snapshot a lockfile: feeding it back rebuilds the same pack. It also answers which model versions were live at resolution time (composition-level time travel), and it remains valid offline for its members' declared freshness windows (last-known-good).

## Failure paths not taken

| Condition | Behavior |
|---|---|
| Task matches no declared routing hint | Hard fail, non-zero exit, no pack; diagnostic reproduces the purpose and referenced entities. |
| Edge endpoint absent from `entries/` | Hard fail, no partial pack; diagnostic names the edge and the missing endpoint. |
| A `requires` minimum disagreeing with the edge `declared_min` | Hard fail at CI, before resolve time. |
| A `references` edge naming a kind absent from the target's `exports` | Hard fail at CI, before resolve time. |
| Source older than its declared `sync_contract` freshness | Pack emitted with a staleness flag and the last-known `observed_at`; the consumer decides. |
| Assembled members exceed `max_tokens` | Degrade members to a terser identifier-plus-summary form, record the degradation in `coverage_statement`. |
| Pinned ref unavailable when a future fetch stage is enabled | Reuse the last-known-good snapshot inside its freshness window, else hard fail. |
